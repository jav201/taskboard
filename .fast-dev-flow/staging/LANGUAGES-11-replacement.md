## INSERT — one new bullet in the commitments list, after the dimension-span bullet

- **A SERIES is an ORDINATE DIMENSION STACK, not an axis.** Every conventional
  terminal plot axis is `│` and `└`, and neither is among this language's ten
  marks — so a chart axis here is unconstructable, and the language draws the
  trace the way a drawing office does instead: **a schedule of ordinate
  dimensions from a common datum.** Each sample is a run from the sheet's left
  terminator, its figure standing on the run, and **the locus of the closing
  terminators IS the curve**. The vertical a plot appears to need is never
  drawn; it is the column the terminators fall in. The scale is **published**,
  as a full-width span figured with the ceiling on the last row — which is what
  lets a series derive its own ceiling without breaking DATAVIZ law 2, since
  the one thing law 2 forbids is a scale the reader cannot see.

## INSERT — the closing paragraph, replacing the deleted one

**What the first outside app taught this language, and what the kit now does
(emersio-lab, 2026-09-04; taskboard batch `kits-learn`, 2026-09-04).** Three
gaps were found by trying to *use* the language rather than by reading it, and
all three are closed: (1) a series is a **dimensioned trace**, decided over a
renunciation because a renunciation must follow from something a language
believes and this one would only have followed from a short alphabet — Ledger
refuses images because *a figure is audited, not shown*, and Blueprint's
doctrine points the other way, at *extents and tolerances* (L-34); (2)
`title_block()` is now a thin adapter over **`stamp(rows, w, strip=None)`**,
which takes content as rows of `(caption, value, knocked)` cells and selection
as an optional extra, so any app with an identity and a state gets the block
and an app with no mode strip gets no registration marks — there is nothing
selected to register (L-32); (3) the `tint` posture **reads its `label`** and
letters it onto a third span above its two, so the sheet states what the pixels
ARE (`60 × 20 CELLS`) above what they MEASURE (`480px`) — paid for out of the
reserved rectangle, so the glass loses the row the caption gains (L-31).

**The portable lesson is the one the three share:** each gap was invisible from
inside the reference app, because the reference app was the only caller. A
language's second consumer is its first test.
