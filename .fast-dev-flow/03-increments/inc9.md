## 1. The decision, and why

**A declared SERIES MECHANISM — the ordinate dimension stack. Not a
renunciation.** Reasoning is in `spec.md` §6.1 and is not repeated in full;
the three loads it carries:

1. **A renunciation must follow from something the language believes.** Ledger
   refuses images because *"a figure is audited, not shown"* — that follows
   from double-entry. Solari refuses because *"one shape, the row; an image
   cannot flip"* — that follows from the machine it imitates. Blueprint has no
   such sentence about series, and its doctrine points the other way: *the
   frame stops CONTAINING and starts MEASURING*, *the only language here where
   the chrome IS the data-viz*, *fits: anything spatial, anything with extents
   and tolerances*. A convergence curve is a sequence of extents. **Renouncing
   plots would have been the first renunciation in this file adopted for a
   short alphabet rather than for a commitment.**
2. **What is unconstructable is an AXIS, and a series is not an axis.** L-34 is
   exactly right that `│` and `└` are unavailable, and nothing here smuggles
   them in (asserted, not promised). But a drawing office does not plot with an
   axis box — it schedules **ordinate dimensions from a common datum**. Every
   sample is a run from the same left terminator, its length the value, its
   figure standing on the run. Stack them and **the locus of the closing
   terminators is the curve**: drawn in `┤`, datum `├`, run `─`, clip flag `╌`.
   The vertical the plot appears to need is never drawn; it is the column the
   terminators fall in.
3. **It fixes the half `dimension()` could not.** Law 2 forbids normalising a
   row to itself, and a kit method handed one row at a time obeys it only by
   declaring a constant. A series is the one case where the siblings are in
   hand — so the ceiling may be derived, and therefore must be **published**.
   The last row is a full-width span figured with the ceiling: a scale bar,
   meaning "this width is that value".

What it renders, at 46 × 10 with a label — the ragged right edge is the trace:

## 2. What changed

`Blueprint.series(values, w, h, ceiling=None, label="")` — exactly `h` rows of
`w` cells, with `_trace_cells()` and `_sample()` beside it.

- **The declared ladder when the seat is short:** the SCALE row never drops (a
  trace whose scale is unstated is the reading law 2 exists to prevent), the
  label goes first, the samples reduce after that.
- **`_sample()` keeps both endpoints.** A trace's endpoints are its datum —
  where the run started and where it got to — so a subsample that dropped
  either would answer a different question from the one a convergence curve
  asks.
- **The figure is DROPPED, not pushed outside** (`outside=False`, a flag
  `_span_text` already had for exactly this). A figure lettered past the
  closing terminator would put ink to the right of the value, and the whole
  reading is that nothing stands right of a terminator except longer samples'
  terminators.
- **`_trace_cells()` is deliberately not `_cells()`.** Same law twice —
  including the microbar floor — because the law is DATAVIZ's; different
  method because `_cells()` takes *integer days* against `SCALE_DAYS` in a
  `SPAN_W` seat, and widening it to floats would have put the board's every due
  date through a code path a convergence curve asked for.
- **The class docstring gained the commitment**, which is what makes §11 true:
  the docstring is what `export_to_skill.py` projects into the skill's
  `DOC[...]`, so the language now *ships* saying it has a series.

**§11 itself is staged, not written.** `LANGUAGES.md` is hand-written and is
not generated by the exporter, so the exact replacement text sits in
`.fast-dev-flow/staging/LANGUAGES-11-replacement.md` — delete-block, insert-
block, ready to apply. **Writing it into the skill is the orchestrator's call
and this batch did not do it.**

## 5. Findings

**F-11 · NEW · a derived scale must be derived from the DATA, not from the
seats that fit.** First implementation read the ceiling off the *subsample*, so
the scale depended on the height of the region: the same series in a shorter
seat came back drawn against a different ceiling, and at one seat the trace's
**last** value rendered full-width — a converged run shown touching full scale.
Caught by the ladder test at h=3, not by reading. **Portable: when a mechanism
both subsamples and normalises, the two must not share an input.**

**A LIMIT of the decision, stated rather than discovered later.** Below the
width its own figure needs, a sample draws as a bare span with **no figure** —
so the tail of a converging trace loses its numbers, which is exactly where a
reader may want them. That is the price of `outside=False`, and the mitigation
is the published scale row: the tail is measured off it, which is how a
drawing is read. If a future consumer needs tail figures, the honest move is a
leader to a schedule beside the trace (the language already has the leader),
**not** letting figures cross their terminators.

**What L-34 got right and what it did not say.** Right: the axis is
unconstructable, `dimension()` is the wrong mechanism, and the choice is the
finding. Not said: **the decision is cheap.** L-34 reads as a standoff between
two large options, and the implementation is one method, two helpers and ten
tests — the cost of *deciding* was the cost of the whole thing. A finding that
frames a choice as expensive can defer it longer than it deserves.
