them. They are equal for a solid glyph and far apart for a
hairline, because most of a hairline's pixels are partway to
the ground and the declared ratio describes a colour that
appears in hardly any of them.

==============================================================================
E. A FLOOR TO PROPOSE, NOT TO ENFORCE
==============================================================================

The 79 meaning marks the corpus draws span 0.053 to 9.802 on coverage x effective.

AND 45 OF THEM ARE UNDER 3:1 ON DECLARED
CONTRAST AT THE SEAT REPORTED HERE. That number is NOT a count
of violations and must not be read as one. `worst seat` is this
report's own choice of where to look, and inc74 judged seats one
at a time: `DIM_CLASSIFIES` has eight seats and three verdicts,
and a mark that is a severity rung on a log row and a decorative
leader in a masthead is legitimately two things. The count is
here because nobody has taken it before, and because it is the
size of the question a ruling would be answering.

WHY THIS PRODUCT AND NOT A RATIO. A ratio cannot say that a mark
holding 4.5:1 is three pixels. Section C's worst rows are the
proof: they are not contrast failures, they are AREA failures
wearing a passing ratio -- and four rounds of this programme had
no way to tell the two apart, because the svg carries a colour
and not a drawing.

WHY IT IS NOT ENFORCED HERE. Three reasons and they are not
excuses:

  1. The product has no published precedent. WCAG 1.4.3 is a
     ratio and 1.4.11 is a ratio; nothing in either standard
     multiplies by area. A floor invented inside an increment
     and asserted in the same increment is a number nobody
     argued with.
  2. Half of what it would fail is DECORATION, and this file
     cannot tell which half. That is round four's §8.4 verbatim
     and inc74's `DIM_CLASSIFIES` is the shape the answer takes
     -- a seat list with verdicts, written by somebody who
     decides, not a threshold.
  3. The face is not the terminal. Windows Terminal draws its
     own box and block glyphs, so every row here whose glyph is
     box drawing is a number about Cascadia's version of it.

WHAT A RULING WOULD NEED TO SAY, in one line each:

  - is coverage x effective the measure, or coverage AND
    effective as two clauses with two floors?
  - does it bind every meaning mark, or only the ones a
    `DIM_CLASSIFIES`-shaped list says classify?
  - is a mark judged at its worst seat or at its declared one?
    Section D reports the worst; the answer changes which kits
    are in the top ten.
